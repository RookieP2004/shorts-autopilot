# 2026-11-21-L · Treatment: "Why Doctors Refused to Wash Their Hands"

Grown from this week's wild-story Short (2026-11-18-a, Semmelweis in 40 s), taken much deeper: the two doors, the
detective work, the bowl, the fall, the proof, and the reflex that still carries his name.

## Three shapes considered
1. **A journey through time** (1846 -> 1867 -> today). Clear, but flat: every stop has the same weight.
2. **A before/after** (18.3% -> 1.2%). Strong number, but the whole film would peak at minute two.
3. **A case that turns** (chosen). Semmelweis works it like a detective: suspects ruled out one by one, a death that
   cracks the case, the fix, then the twist: the cure is right and still rejected. The turn moves the question from
   "what kills the mothers?" to "why won't people believe the answer?", which is the part a viewer keeps.

**Logline:** In 1846 Vienna, mothers beg not to enter one hospital door; a young doctor finds the killer on his
colleagues' own hands and cuts deaths tenfold with a bowl of chlorine water, but medicine pushes him out, until germ
theory proves him right after he is gone.

## Benchmarks (grammar only, never their material)
- Classic TV history documentaries on medicine (archival reconstruction + evidence board + talking presenter): learn
  the slow drift on stills, the typed lower thirds and the held silence before a death. Never take their footage,
  narration or graphics.
- Lemo style donor: *visual investigation* (grammar only: suspects pinned to a board and crossed out one by one,
  string from evidence to conclusion). Rebuilt with our own `paper`, `marker`, `string` pieces on the cork board.

## Native move at the emotional peak
The held silence after Kolletschka's death: the dissection hall (night set) holds, Ved breathes, then red string runs
on the cork board from the autopsy-room photo, along a pair of hands, to the First Clinic's ward photo, and the stamp
THE HANDS lands as the loudest moment of the film. The case file literally connects the two rooms of the hospital.

## Ending echoes the opening
The film opens on a mother afraid of a door; it ends on Ved by the cafe door in today's town, the washbasin bowl
beside him: "The hardest thing to wash off is *we've always done it this way*."
